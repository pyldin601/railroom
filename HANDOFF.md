## User preferences

- Prefer natural recorded textures; avoid obvious repeating up/down loops. User later explicitly allowed synthesis/FM for traction and approved the synthesized motor.
- Ukrainian passenger-train character: metallic rolling and clear metal-on-metal clacks with rail resonance.
- Keep individual wheels spatially distinct. Pitch differences are small and fixed, up to approximately ±1%; rolling pitch must not change with speed.
- The occupied carriage must remain loudest. External wheels still differ by their physical distance; avoid flat volume across a carriage or abrupt steps between external carriages.
- Distant metal should carry farther than the body of the clack. Train ends should be barely audible through a smooth fade, not sudden muting of whole carriages.
- Ambient carriage sound should be quiet and contain no speech.
- Smooth acceleration/braking and low CPU use are important for relaxed listening.

- Route length 64 km, starts at Kyiv-Pasazhyrskyi and ends at Fastiv I.

There are 43 direct string joins and 10 connector groups: 1–5 × 12.5 m or 1–2 × 25 m rails. See TRACK-LAYOUT.md for placements.
- **Internal welds are completely silent**: retained in route data, skipped by crossing scheduling and rejected by the mixer. String-end joints still produce impacts. Rolling continues over welds.
- 25 m jointed areas: 0–1,500; 6,250–7,750; 14,000–17,000; 34,250–35,750; 42,000–45,000; 62,500–64,000 m. Additional 25 m jointed areas: 23,250–25,250; 51,250–53,250; 60,500–62,500 m. Consecutive equal limits are merged into one interval with `sectionIds`; only actual speed changes produce markers. Autopilot follows these markers; manual driving can exceed them.

## Current audio

### Wheel impacts and metal hiss

Relevant files: `src/audio/spatial-mixer.js`, `impact-distance.js`, `metal-hiss.js`.

- All 80 wheels can produce impacts. Budget: **1024 simultaneous impact groups**, each with two synchronized sample sources (direct clack and processed metal). Horn and power voices also occupy the shared tracked voice set.
- Clacks use eight alternate recorded, processed one-second samples: `public/audio/joint-*-rail-reverb.wav`. They already contain modeled rail resonance. See `AUDIO-LICENSES.md` for source attribution and processing.
- Metal hiss uses the **same clack**, high-pass at **4 kHz**, with **500 ms RT60 reverb**, 35% wet addition. Rendered once per decoded recording and cached; no per-hit synthesis of the effect.
- Metal branch base gain is **0.7**, twice its earlier 0.35. Overall impact and separate metal sliders both affect it.
- Metal impact distance reach is **4× original**. Direct impact reach is unchanged.
- The exact gain implementation in `impact-distance.js` is authoritative. It uses wheel-to-seat distance and continuous exponential attenuation. External transmission multipliers are up to 0.6 direct / 0.9 metal, reduced as necessary so the nearest external wheel is below 90% of the quietest occupied-carriage wheel.
- `endHissGain` adds smooth attenuation toward both physical train ends over three coach lengths. It uses smoothstep from 0.1 at an outermost wheel to 1 in the interior. It never reduces the occupied carriage. This replaced abrupt 10% settings on carriages 1/2/9/10, which the user disliked.
- Seat changes smoothly update emitter gains. Cancellations stop both clack and metal tail; finished sources are reclaimed.
- Clicking a wheel on the train canvas plays that wheel's clack plus metal with its distance/spatial settings. Works while stopped, using a 72 km/h audition level when speed is zero.

### Rolling

Relevant files: `src/audio/rolling.js`, `rolling-bands.js`.

- Only the **eight wheels of the occupied carriage** produce rolling. Other carriages currently contribute impacts, not rolling.
- Recorded narrow metallic rolling texture, split at **500 Hz** into low and high layers with complementary fourth-order filters.
- Each wheel has its own loop phase and fixed pitch offset. Cents: `[-17, 7, 15, -8, 11, -14, -4, 10]`. No speed-driven rolling pitch changes.
- Both bands share each wheel's distance gain and 120 ms smoothing.
- Rolling uses the metal distance curve with its **original reach of 1**, not the later 4× reach or 0.7 impact-effect gain. End-hiss attenuation does not apply to rolling.
- Base rolling amplitude was increased 2× earlier; current speed envelope contains `.28`. Retain the existing normalization unless deliberately retuning it.

### Traction, horn, brakes and ambience

- Defaults: master 100%, traction 30%, carriage ambience 10%; impact/metal/rolling/rolling-low/rolling-high/braking sliders 100%.
- Master mix boost 1.4; compressor provides headroom control.
- Traction: continuous synthesized motor plus quieter Taurus-inspired PWM notes. A recorded motor-texture option remains. PWM was reduced 3× after initial addition.
- Taurus PWM uses a designed held-note sequence; its precise tuning/speed thresholds are not verified Siemens measurements.
- Locomotive sounds use the front-positioned filtered `LocomotiveSpace` bus with short spatial reflections, avoiding a sound centred in the listener's head.
- Horn button / H shortcut: **synthesized two-tone pneumatic horn**, not a real Siemens recording. Blast about 1.3 s; current reverb **2.4 s RT60**, 2.8 s tail space, 65% dry / 70% wet gains, eight delays, four diffusion stages, 1.8 kHz wet low-pass.
- Horn distance gain is `1 / (1 + distanceToLocomotive / 28)`, about 20.5% (−13.8 dB) at the default ten-carriage seat, before existing locomotive-bus attenuation. Applies to manual and automatic horn.
- Real Siemens horn search found a Vectron depot-horn video (`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ePI7YYaTfEA`) and Taurus model-decoder sample listings. No reusable Taurus recording was verified or installed. Don't claim the current horn is authentic recorded Siemens audio.
- Friction braking separates recorded hiss from a speed-dependent tonal layer to avoid repeating pitch sweeps.
- Pneumatic model includes brake-cylinder pressure, reservoir and compressor. Cylinder release uses a 1.4 s exponential time constant, so the release hiss fades as pressure falls. Compressor hysteresis 7.2–9 bar; initial reservoir 8.5 bar. This is a designed approximation.
- Quiet procedural cabin ambience has no people talking.

## Controls and autopilot

- Autopilot follows route speed limits, anticipates braking, visits all stops, dwells 60 simulation seconds and uses mild controls. Manual throttle or service-brake interaction disables it; other manual navigation/control actions also disable it where implemented.
- Autopilot horns once before the initial stationary departure and after intermediate dwells, holding for 1.4 seconds before proceeding. No departure horn at terminus or when autopilot is enabled while already moving.
- Pause freezes simulation time. Neutral-section power limits apply to autopilot too.
- Simulation and audio scheduling are tied to the audio clock; avoid moving scheduling to animation-frame timing.
- Full-route autopilot tests cover all stops, dwell duration, gentle braking and speed limits. Existing late-engagement/overspeed constraints remain; this is not railway safety software.
